1. DISCRIMINATION — AUC, GINI, KS
================================================================

  Model        AUC      Gini     KS       KS occurs at PD
  -----------  -------  -------  -------  ---------------
  Scorecard    0.7424   0.4848   0.3639   0.0782
  Challenger   0.7847   0.5694   0.4365   0.0665

--- INTERPRETATION ---
KS (Kolmogorov-Smirnov) measures the maximum separation between
the cumulative distributions of goods and bads. It is the metric
credit risk practitioners reach for after Gini.

Conventional retail scorecard bands:
  KS < 0.20        weak / unusable
  KS 0.20 - 0.30   marginal
  KS 0.30 - 0.40   acceptable
  KS > 0.40        strong

Scorecard at 0.364 is comfortably acceptable.
Challenger at 0.437 is strong.

--- OBSERVATION: KS LOCATION ---
Both KS maxima occur very close to the population bad rate
(0.0807): scorecard at 0.0782, challenger at 0.0665.

This is not coincidental. Maximum separation between the good
and bad cumulative distributions tends to sit near the base rate
when a model is well calibrated. It is a soft confirmation that
the Phase 6 decision to train UNWEIGHTED produced a sensible
probability scale. A model trained with is_unbalance would show
its KS maximum shifted far from the true base rate.

--- WHY BRIER AND CALIBRATION DISAGREE ---
Brier score is a PROPER SCORING RULE. It conflates two distinct
properties:
    Brier = calibration error + refinement (discrimination)

The challenger's superior discrimination (Gini 0.569 vs 0.485)
lowers its Brier even though its band-by-band probability
estimates are less faithful. Brier is a measure of overall
probabilistic quality, NOT a pure calibration metric.

PRACTICAL RULE:
  Brier / logloss        -> overall probabilistic quality
  Calibration curve, MAD -> calibration specifically
Quoting Brier alone would have produced the wrong conclusion
here.

--- WHY THE SCORECARD CALIBRATES BETTER ---
WoE-transformed logistic regression fits log-odds directly on a
linearised feature space. The link function IS the probability
model — calibration is structural.

LightGBM optimises a loss that produces good rankings;
calibration emerges as a by-product rather than being
guaranteed by construction. The visible wobble around PD 0.05
to 0.15 is the consequence.

--- WHY THIS MATTERS COMMERCIALLY ---
This is a substantive argument for the scorecard BEYOND
interpretability. For IFRS 9 / CECL provisioning, PD accuracy
within each stage bucket drives the provision calculation
directly — correct ordering alone is insufficient. The
scorecard's tighter calibration has direct financial
consequence.

--- SHARED BEHAVIOUR ---
Both models under-predict slightly in the top band (ratios
0.954 and 0.954) — the highest-risk applicants default a little
less often than predicted. Conservative in the safe direction,
and a common pattern in credit models.

4. DECISION THRESHOLD — THREE APPROACHES
================================================================
Class imbalance was deliberately NOT handled during training
(Phase 6, section 4). The operating point is set here instead.

--- COST ASSUMPTIONS (EXPLICIT AND TESTABLE) ---
  Cost of approving a bad loan (FN)      = 100
  Cost of declining a good applicant (FP) =  10
  Ratio 10:1 — a common starting assumption for unsecured
  retail lending.

This ratio is an ASSUMPTION, not a fact. In a live project it
would be derived from actual loss-given-default and
profit-per-account figures. Stating it explicitly makes the
result testable rather than authoritative.

--- RESULTS ---
model       method               threshold  approval  bad_rate  bads    goods   total
                                            rate      approved  caught  lost    cost
----------  -------------------  ---------  --------  --------  ------  ------  -------
Scorecard   KS-maximising          0.0782    0.6536    0.0394   0.6810  0.3170  337,640
Scorecard   Cost-optimal (10:1)    0.0889    0.7019    0.0427   0.6284  0.2691  336,660
Scorecard   90% approval           0.1766    0.9000    0.0621   0.3074  0.0818  390,150
Challenger  KS-maximising          0.0665    0.6265    0.0290   0.7748  0.3383  303,070
Challenger  Cost-optimal (10:1)    0.0791    0.6836    0.0335   0.7164  0.2812  299,810
Challenger  90% approval           0.1883    0.9000    0.0576   0.3579  0.0774  362,540

  Baseline — approve everyone:  cost 496,500,  bad rate 8.07%

--- FINDING 1: EVERY CONFIGURATION BEATS APPROVING EVERYONE ---
Best configuration (challenger, cost-optimal) reduces cost from
496,500 to 299,810 — a 40% reduction.

--- FINDING 2: KS AND COST-OPTIMAL CONVERGE ---
  Scorecard : threshold 0.0782 vs 0.0889, cost 337,640 vs 336,660
              (0.3% apart)
  Challenger: threshold 0.0665 vs 0.0791, cost 303,070 vs 299,810

This convergence is structural, not accidental. KS maximises
separation between the good and bad distributions, and at a
10:1 cost ratio the optimal cut-off falls close to that point.
At a 50:1 ratio the two would diverge sharply — the cost-optimal
threshold would move much lower to catch more bads.

--- FINDING 3: THE APPROVAL CONSTRAINT IS EXPENSIVE BUT REAL ---
Forcing 90% approval raises scorecard cost from 336,660 to
390,150 (+16%), because applicants the model flags must be
accepted.

But goods_lost falls from 26.9% to 8.2%. This is the volume-
versus-loss tension a credit committee actually negotiates.
Under a pure cost lens the model would decline ~30% of
applicants; a lender trying to grow the book will not accept
that.

--- FINDING 4: BOOK QUALITY AT EQUAL APPROVAL RATE ---
The cleanest statement of the challenger's advantage:

  At 90% approval    Bad rate in book   Defaults avoided
  -----------------  ----------------   ----------------
  No model                 8.07%              0.0%
  Scorecard                6.21%             30.7%
  Challenger               5.76%             35.8%

The challenger delivers a 0.45 percentage point better book at
identical volume. On a 100,000-loan portfolio averaging EUR
10,000 per loan, that is roughly 450 fewer defaults.

--- RECOMMENDATION ---
Adopt the 90% APPROVAL THRESHOLD as the primary operating point,
reporting cost-optimal as a sensitivity analysis.

Reasoning: the cost ratio is an unvalidated assumption, whereas
approval rate is a genuine business constraint that a lender
sets directly. Presenting cost-optimal as THE answer would be
over-claiming on a self-supplied input.

  Scorecard  operating threshold: PD >= 0.1766 -> decline
  Challenger operating threshold: PD >= 0.1883 -> decline

  Top 5 by PSI:
    CC_MEAN_UTILISATION              0.00041

    AMT_GOODS_PRICE                  0.00023
    MEAN_AMT_CREDIT_SUM              0.00022
    ENTRANCES_AVG                    0.00021

--- WHY NEAR-ZERO PSI IS THE POINT, NOT A LIMITATION ---
Development and holdout are random splits of the same
population, so near-zero PSI is the EXPECTED result. Its value
is as a CALIBRATED NOISE FLOOR.

Random sampling variation on this population produces PSI of
approximately 0.0002 to 0.0004. When the model scores new
applicants six months from now, a PSI of 0.05 would be roughly
100x the sampling noise and worth investigating — even though
0.05 sits well below the conventional 0.10 alert threshold.

Without this baseline there would be no way to distinguish
"slightly elevated but normal" from "something has genuinely
changed".

--- STATED LIMITATION ---
This is a WITHIN-SAMPLE stability check, not out-of-time
validation. Both populations are random splits of the same
snapshot. Genuine production monitoring would compare against
a later time period, where macroeconomic shifts, portfolio mix
changes and policy changes drive real drift.

This is a limitation of the available data, not of the method.
Stating it explicitly is preferable to implying the model has
been proven stable over time.

--- PRODUCTION MONITORING RECOMMENDATION ---
  Monitor monthly:
    - Score PSI vs development baseline (alert above 0.10)
    - Feature-level PSI (identifies WHICH input drifted)
    - Observed vs predicted bad rate by score band
    - Approval rate vs target
  Feature-level PSI matters because score drift alone tells you
  something moved but not what; feature PSI is diagnosable and
  actionable.

--- THE TRADE, STATED PLAINLY ---
The challenger wins on every discrimination metric. The gap is
0.0423 AUC / 0.0846 Gini, worth 0.45pp of book quality at equal
approval rate.

The scorecard wins on calibration accuracy, interpretability,
and regulatory defensibility — and calibration is not a
secondary concern where PD feeds provisioning.

The purpose of the dual-track design was never to declare a
winner but to QUANTIFY the cost of interpretability so that a
credit committee can make an informed decision rather than an
assumed one.
