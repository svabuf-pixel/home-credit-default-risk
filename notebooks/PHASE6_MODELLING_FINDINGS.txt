1. VALIDATION STRATEGY — THE CENTRAL DECISION OF THE PHASE
================================================================

--- THE PROBLEM ---
All target-aware preprocessing in Phases 4-5 (binning, WoE
encoding, median imputation, winsorisation caps, IV-based feature
selection) was fitted on the FULL training set. That is correct
for SCREENING, but if a model is then cross-validated on those
same pre-computed WoE values, every validation fold has already
seen its own target through the encoding. The resulting CV
estimate is optimistically biased.

--- OPTIONS CONSIDERED ---
A. Full nested pipeline — refit binning, WoE, imputation,
   winsorisation, VARCLUS and IV selection inside every fold.
   Methodologically pure, but slow, and the feature set would
   differ per fold, making the "final" scorecard ambiguous.

B. Freeze feature selection, refit encoding per fold — keep the
   27 features fixed, but refit BinningProcess inside each fold.
   Treats feature selection as an analyst decision and encoding
   as a fitted transformation that must respect fold boundaries.

C. Use pre-computed WoE as-is — fastest, knowingly optimistic.

--- DECISION: OPTION B PLUS A GENUINE HOLDOUT ---
1. Carve a 20% holdout from the training data BEFORE any
   modelling. Never touched until final evaluation.
2. On the remaining 80% (development set), run 5-fold CV with
   WoE refitted inside each fold.
3. Report the holdout figure as the honest performance estimate.

Note: the Kaggle application_test.csv cannot serve as a holdout —
it has no TARGET, so nothing can be scored against it.

--- THE SPLIT ---
  Development set : 246,008 rows   bad rate 0.0807
  Holdout set     :  61,503 rows   bad rate 0.0807
  Stratified on TARGET, random_state=42, IDs persisted to
  holdout_split.pkl for reproducibility and audit.

Events per variable on development: 19,860 bads / 24 features
= 828. Conventional minimum is 10-20. Ample data.

--- WHY THIS MATTERED ---
CV → holdout drop was only ~0.004 AUC for BOTH models
(scorecard 0.7467 → 0.7424; challenger 0.7881 → 0.7847).
Had the pre-computed WoE been reused for CV, the CV figure
would have been inflated and the holdout drop far larger.
The discipline is validated by the result.

--- ACKNOWLEDGED LIMITATION ---
VARCLUS and VIF are not target-aware and do not require
refolding. However the IV-based cluster-representative
selection IS target-aware. Strictly, the entire selection
pipeline should be re-run per fold for a fully unbiased
estimate. The holdout provides the honest number that
compensates for this.

2. SCORECARD TRACK — LOGISTIC REGRESSION ON WoE
================================================================
Input : 27 features from Phase 5, RAW values (not pre-WoE)
Method: BinningProcess refitted per fold, LogisticRegression
        with no regularisation, no class weighting

--- CONFIGURATION RATIONALE ---
No regularisation (C=np.inf): a regulatory scorecard requires
raw maximum-likelihood coefficients. Regularisation shrinks
coefficients in ways that make the points-per-bin table harder
to justify. Overfitting is already controlled by feature
selection (247 → 24) and the VIF check.

No class weighting: WoE-transformed logistic regression on the
natural class distribution produces well-calibrated PDs
directly, which is what credit risk requires. (See section 3
for the empirical evidence.)

--- INITIAL RESULT: 27 FEATURES ---
  Fold 1  train 0.7503   valid 0.7480
  Fold 2  train 0.7501   valid 0.7482
  Fold 3  train 0.7507   valid 0.7446
  Fold 4  train 0.7509   valid 0.7439
  Fold 5  train 0.7496   valid 0.7502
  Mean valid AUC 0.7470 (+/- 0.0027)   Gini 0.4940

Train-validation gap is ~0.002 across all folds — a stable,
non-overfit model.

--- NOTE ON FOLD COUNT ---
Considered increasing beyond 5 folds. Rejected: more folds
improve the PRECISION OF THE ESTIMATE, not the model itself.
Standard deviation was already 0.0027; more folds would give
0.747 +/- 0.002 instead of +/- 0.003, with an identical model.
AUC is lifted by better features or a better algorithm, not
by resampling more finely.

3. COEFFICIENT DIAGNOSTICS AND STEPWISE PRUNING
================================================================

--- SIGN CONVENTION (initially misread) ---
An initial reading flagged 25 of 27 coefficients as
"wrong-signed" because they were negative. That was based on
the assumption that WoE is constructed so higher = lower risk,
giving positive coefficients.

optbinning uses the OPPOSITE convention: WoE = log(event /
non-event), so HIGHER WoE = HIGHER risk... but the bin tables
confirm the practical effect is that the lower-event-rate bin
receives POSITIVE WoE:

  EMERGENCYSTATE_MODE bin table:
    (-inf, inf)   event rate 7.00%   WoE +0.1538
    Missing       event rate 9.26%   WoE -0.1503

So higher WoE = lower risk, and the EXPECTED coefficient sign
is NEGATIVE. That 25 of 27 shared the same sign was the
consistency one wants, not a defect.

CORRECTED RULE FOR THIS PROJECT:
  negative coefficient = correct
  positive coefficient = suspect (sign reversal)

Lesson: always verify sign convention against an actual bin
table rather than assuming the library's definition.

--- GENUINE ISSUES FOUND (27-feature model) ---
Pseudo R-squared 0.1123

  WRONG SIGN (positive coefficient):
    EXT_2_3_STD          coef +0.3632   p 0.0000   (significant)
    NONLIVINGAREA_AVG    coef +0.1604   p 0.2055   (not sig)

  NOT SIGNIFICANT (p >= 0.05):
    NONLIVINGAREA_AVG    p 0.2055
    EXT_SOURCE_1         coef -0.0083   p 0.7298

--- DIAGNOSIS ---
EXT_SOURCE_1 is the notable case. It has IV 0.146, ranks 20th
by LightGBM gain, and is a known strong univariate predictor —
yet its coefficient collapses to effectively zero (-0.008) in
the multivariate model.

This is conditional redundancy: EXT_1_2_3_MIN and EXT_1_3_MEAN
already contain EXT_SOURCE_1's information, leaving nothing
unique for it to explain. VIF (1.42) did not catch this
because VIF measures LINEAR DEPENDENCE, not CONDITIONAL
CONTRIBUTION. The two diagnostics are complementary.

EXT_2_3_STD flipping sign is classic suppression — fine
univariately (IV 0.039) but reverses once the EXT_SOURCE
level features enter the model.

--- ACTION: DROPPED 3 FEATURES ---
  EXT_SOURCE_1       — no conditional contribution
  NONLIVINGAREA_AVG  — no contribution AND wrong sign
  EXT_2_3_STD        — wrong sign (suppression)

--- RESULT: 24-FEATURE MODEL ---
  Pseudo R-squared  0.1118  (from 0.1123)
  Development AUC   0.7498
  Non-significant   0
  Wrong sign        0

Re-run cross-validation:
  Fold 1  0.7473    Fold 2  0.7480    Fold 3  0.7445
  Fold 4  0.7438    Fold 5  0.7500

  27 features -> mean valid AUC 0.7470 (+/- 0.0027)
  24 features -> mean valid AUC 0.7467 (+/- 0.0023)

Difference of 0.0003 — well inside the fold variation and
statistically indistinguishable. Three features removed at no
measurable cost, producing a model where every coefficient is
significant and correctly signed, and fold stability slightly
improved.

For a regulatory scorecard this is an unambiguous improvement:
simpler to validate, simpler to explain, and no coefficient a
reviewer can challenge.

4. CHALLENGER TRACK — CALIBRATION EXPERIMENT
================================================================
Purpose: test the Phase 5 hypothesis that is_unbalance=True
lifts ranking but destroys probability calibration.

Method: identical 5-fold CV, identical base parameters, only
the class-weighting flag varied.

  Config          AUC       LogLoss    Brier
  --------------  --------  ---------  --------
  unweighted      0.7852    0.2377     0.06604
  is_unbalance    0.7841    0.4976     0.16560

--- FINDING: HYPOTHESIS CONFIRMED ---
AUC difference is 0.0011 — within fold noise. Class reweighting
bought nothing in ranking quality.

Calibration is 2.5x worse. Brier rises from 0.066 to 0.166;
logloss more than doubles.

--- MECHANISM ---
is_unbalance reweights the bad class by approximately 11.4x
(the good:bad ratio), so the model optimises as though the base
rate were ~50% rather than 8.07%. Its output probabilities are
calibrated to that synthetic population, not the real one — it
may predict 45% for an applicant whose true PD is 12%. Rankings
survive because relative order is preserved; absolute
probabilities become meaningless.

--- WHY THIS MATTERS IN CREDIT RISK SPECIFICALLY ---
PD feeds directly into:
  - Expected Loss = PD x LGD x EAD
  - IFRS 9 / CECL provisioning
  - Risk-based pricing
A model predicting 45% where the true PD is 12% would overstate
provisions by nearly 4x. Ranking-only performance is
insufficient; calibrated probabilities are a hard requirement.

--- DECISION ---
Train UNWEIGHTED. Handle class imbalance at the DECISION
THRESHOLD in Phase 7 rather than by distorting the training
objective.

BENCHMARK: predicting the base rate (0.0807) for every applicant
gives Brier 0.0742. Both final models beat this; the
is_unbalance config (0.1656) is more than twice as bad as doing
nothing.

5. CHALLENGER TRACK — OPTUNA HYPERPARAMETER TUNING
================================================================
Trials : 20 (100 model fits — 5-fold CV per trial)
Runtime: ~2.5 hours
Objective: maximise mean 5-fold CV AUC

  Baseline (untuned)  CV AUC 0.7852
  Best trial          CV AUC 0.7881
  Gain                       0.0029  (~0.6 Gini points)

--- BEST PARAMETERS ---
  learning_rate      0.0254
  num_leaves         49
  max_depth          4
  min_child_samples  254
  subsample          0.748
  colsample_bytree   0.467
  reg_alpha          0.207
  reg_lambda         1.307

--- INTERPRETATION ---
Every chosen parameter pushes toward MORE REGULARISATION and
LESS COMPLEXITY:
  - max_depth=4 binds hard (a depth-4 tree holds at most 16
    leaves, so num_leaves=49 is never reached) — Optuna
    effectively selected depth-4 stumps
  - min_child_samples=254 requires large leaf populations
  - colsample_bytree=0.467 shows each tree under half the features
  - learning_rate halved from the baseline

This is the signature of a dataset where signal is BROAD AND
SHALLOW rather than deep and interaction-heavy — consistent
with the Phase 5 finding that 215 of 247 features carried
non-zero gain with no single feature dominating after the
EXT_SOURCE block.

It also implies limited headroom in further tuning: the
optimiser is asking for a simpler model, not a more elaborate
one. Additional gains would come from feature engineering or
ensembling, not hyperparameters.

Final model: best iteration 1452 (early stopping on an inner
15% split of the development set; the holdout remained
untouched).

6. BUG FOUND: POSITIONAL MISALIGNMENT IN HOLDOUT COMPARISON
================================================================

--- SYMPTOM ---
First holdout evaluation returned:
  Scorecard   AUC 0.7424
  Challenger  AUC 0.4987   <-- exactly random

A model scoring 0.788 in cross-validation cannot predict at
chance level on data from the same distribution.

--- DIAGNOSIS ---
  y_holdout rows : 61,503     yc_hold rows : 61,503
  Bad rate        : 0.0807     0.0807
  Same IDs, same order : FALSE
  Challenger vs its own aligned target : AUC 0.7847

The two holdout sets contained the SAME APPLICANTS in
DIFFERENT ROW ORDER:
  - y_holdout came from train_test_split on the preprocessed
    frame, retaining the original DataFrame index order
  - yc_hold came from a boolean .isin() mask on the challenger
    frame, returning rows in that frame's own order

So p_challenger[i] and y_holdout[i] referred to different
applicants, collapsing AUC to chance.

--- FIX ---
Align predictions by SK_ID_CURR via an explicit merge rather
than trusting positional order:

  sc_pred = DataFrame({SK_ID_CURR, p_scorecard, TARGET})
  ch_pred = DataFrame({SK_ID_CURR, p_challenger})
  comparison = sc_pred.merge(ch_pred, on="SK_ID_CURR")

--- LESSON ---
Positional alignment between separately-constructed DataFrames
is a silent failure mode — no exception is raised, only
meaningless output. The diagnostic tell was the value itself:
AUC of EXACTLY 0.4987 indicates random pairing, not weak
signal. Had it returned 0.62 the cause could have been
mistaken for feature drift or overfitting and cost hours.

Always merge on a key when combining predictions from
separately-built datasets.

--- THE INTERPRETABILITY COST: 8.5 GINI POINTS ---
This is the commercially meaningful number. The scorecard
surrenders 0.0846 Gini in exchange for:
  - 24 explainable features instead of 247
  - monotonic bins with documented cut-points
  - every coefficient significant and correctly signed
  - a points-per-bin table (Phase 8) that supports
    adverse-action reasoning and regulatory review

Whether that trade is worth making is a business decision.
Being able to QUANTIFY it rather than assert it is the
contribution of the dual-track design.

--- CALIBRATION ---

  Naive (predict base rate for all)  Brier 0.0742
  Scorecard                          Brier 0.06905
  Challenger                         Brier 0.06626
  is_unbalance config (rejected)     Brier 0.1656

Both beat the naive benchmark. The rejected reweighted config
would have been more than twice as bad as predicting nothing.
The unweighted decision is empirically vindicated.

11. OPEN ITEMS FOR PHASE 7
================================================================
1. DECISION THRESHOLD. Class imbalance was deliberately not
   handled during training. Phase 7 must select an operating
   threshold — via KS statistic, cost-based optimisation, or a
   target approval rate.

2. KS STATISTIC AND SCORE BANDS. AUC/Gini measure global
   separation. Credit risk also requires KS (maximum separation
   between cumulative good and bad distributions) and bad rate
   by score decile to confirm monotonic risk ranking across
   the score range.

3. CALIBRATION CURVES. Brier scores are good in aggregate, but
   reliability plots are needed to confirm calibration holds
   across the full PD range, not merely on average.

4. CC_FLAG_EVER_OVER_LIMIT. Flagged in Phase 5 for zero
   LightGBM gain (redundant with CC_MEAN_UTILISATION). It
   survived the scorecard pruning with a significant, correctly
   signed coefficient, so it was retained. Monitor its
   stability in Phase 7 score-band analysis.

5. POPULATION STABILITY (PSI). Compare score distributions
   between development and holdout to establish the baseline
   monitoring metric for production.
