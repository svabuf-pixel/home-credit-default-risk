1. POINTS-BASED SCORECARD — SCALING
================================================================

--- PARAMETERS CHOSEN ---
  PDO (Points to Double the Odds) : 20
  Base score                      : 600
  Base odds at base score         : 50:1 (good:bad)

  factor = PDO / ln(2)                    = 28.8539
  offset = BASE_SCORE - factor * ln(50)   = 487.1229
  intercept (alpha)                       = from LogisticRegression
  n features                              = 24

--- POINTS FORMULA ---
  points(bin) = -(beta_j * WoE_bin + alpha/n) * factor + offset/n

The NEGATIVE SIGN flips direction so that HIGHER SCORE = LOWER
RISK, the universal convention (FICO, VantageScore). The
intercept and offset are divided across features so the
individual point allocations sum to the correct total score.

--- VALIDATION 1: AUC PRESERVATION ---
  AUC from points score : 0.7424
  AUC from model PD     : 0.7424
Points are a monotonic transformation of log-odds, so they MUST
reproduce the model's AUC exactly. Any discrepancy would signal
an arithmetic error in the factor/offset derivation.

--- VALIDATION 2: PDO SCALE VERIFICATION (independent check) ---
From the score-to-PD lookup table, good:bad odds by score band:

  Score band   Good:bad odds
  550 - 559        9.90
  570 - 579       20.18
  590 - 599       38.36
  610 - 619       80.24

Each 20-point step approximately DOUBLES the odds
(9.90 -> 20.18 -> 38.36 -> 80.24), exactly as PDO=20 specifies.

This is a stronger validation than the AUC check: AUC confirms
the ranking is preserved, but the odds progression confirms the
SCALE means what it claims. A scorecard whose PDO does not hold
empirically is misleading to everyone who uses it.

These are STATISTICALLY SIGNIFICANT (they survived the Phase 6
pruning on p-value and sign) but OPERATIONALLY NEGLIGIBLE. That
distinction is worth carrying: statistical significance on
246,008 rows is easy to achieve and says nothing about practical
materiality.

A future simplification exercise could remove all seven with
minimal performance loss, producing a 17-feature card. Retained
here because they cost nothing and each has a defensible
business rationale.

--- RESOLVES PHASE 5 / PHASE 7 OPEN ITEM ---
CC_FLAG_EVER_OVER_LIMIT was flagged in Phase 5 for ZERO LightGBM
gain (redundant with CC_MEAN_UTILISATION) and carried forward for
confirmation. Its 4-point swing versus CC_MEAN_UTILISATION's 12
confirms the redundancy from a third independent angle. All
three diagnostics agree.

--- UNEXPECTED: YEARS_BIRTH AT 3 POINTS ---
Age is normally a material scorecard variable. IV was 0.086 and
LightGBM ranked it 10th by gain, yet it barely moves the score
in the multivariate WoE model. Most plausible explanation:
YEARS_EMPLOYED (12-point swing) and DAYS_SINCE_FIRST_CREDIT
already capture the underlying life-stage signal, leaving age
little unique contribution.

--- MONOTONICITY BREAK AT THE TOP — AND WHY IT IS NOT A DEFECT ---
Bands 620-629 (odds 118.8) and 630-639 (odds 74.5) reverse.
Sample sizes are 719 and 151, with 6 and 2 bads respectively.
With 151 observations a single extra default swings the rate
materially. This is SAMPLING NOISE, not model failure.

Standard practice is to CAP the published scorecard range where
volumes thin out. Recommendation: publish 630+ as a single
top band rather than pretending to resolve differences there.

--- OPERATIONAL READING ---
  Below 500     0.7% of book, 37-60% bad rate — auto-decline zone
  Score 535     Phase 7's 90%-approval threshold; ~15.5% below
  550 - 590     the dense middle, 36% -> 88% cumulative;
                cut-off placement here has the largest volume
                consequences
  Above 600     4.7% of book, under 1.6% bad rate — super-prime,
                candidates for preferential pricing

--- WHY AGREEMENT IS LOWER THAN THE PHASE 5 GAIN COMPARISON ---
Phase 5's LightGBM gain cross-check showed 14 of 27 scorecard
features in the top 30. SHAP shows only 6 of 24 in the top 20.
This is expected, not concerning:

SHAP measures AVERAGE MARGINAL CONTRIBUTION across the model,
which rewards features the tree uses frequently in INTERACTIONS.
The scorecard selected on STANDALONE MONOTONIC SIGNAL after
correlation clustering.

Clearest illustration: INST_COUNT_TOTAL ranks 3rd by SHAP with
an IV of just 0.0029. It contributes almost nothing on its own
but appears constantly in interaction splits.

--- FINDING: CODE_GENDER RANKS 5TH ---
This is the most consequential result of the SHAP analysis.

CODE_GENDER is the 5th most influential feature in the
challenger (mean |SHAP| 0.1094). The scorecard excluded it in
Phase 5 — VARCLUS cluster 21 selected EXT_SOURCE_1 over
CODE_GENDER on IV (0.146 vs 0.039).

Gender is a PROTECTED CHARACTERISTIC under EU anti-
discrimination law and the US Equal Credit Opportunity Act
(ECOA). A production model in which gender is the 5th strongest
driver would face serious regulatory challenge regardless of
its predictive validity.

The individual explanations make this concrete: for the LOWEST
RISK applicant examined, CODE_GENDER = M contributed +0.186 to
the log-odds — actively pushing an otherwise excellent applicant
toward decline on the basis of sex.

The scorecard's exclusion of gender was arrived at INCIDENTALLY
through IV-based cluster representative selection, not by
design. It is nonetheless a genuine advantage, and it argues for
adding an EXPLICIT PROTECTED-ATTRIBUTE EXCLUSION step to the
pipeline rather than relying on selection to remove such
features by chance.

--- NOTE ON READING THE BEESWARM PLOT ---
Categorical features (CODE_GENDER, ORGANIZATION_TYPE,
NAME_EDUCATION_TYPE, NAME_FAMILY_STATUS) appear GREY because
SHAP cannot map categories to a continuous colour gradient.
Only their spread is interpretable, not the value-to-impact
direction.

--- THE DIFFERENCE, STATED PLAINLY ---
SHAP is honest and complete but frequently unusable as customer
communication:

  POSITIVE_DAYS_REGISTRATION = 2 contributes +0.36 log-odds.
  What does one tell the applicant? "Your registration was
  updated 2 days before you applied"?

  MAX_AMT_CREDIT_MAX_OVERDUE = 75,627 is the WINSORISATION CAP
  applied in Phase 4. The true value was higher and was clipped.
  Explaining a preprocessing artefact to a customer is not
  possible.

Reason codes are specific, actionable, and tell the applicant
what to change.

CONCLUSION: SHAP explains THE MODEL TO A DATA SCIENTIST. Reason
codes explain THE DECISION TO THE CUSTOMER. Regulation requires
the second. Post-hoc explainability is a genuine tool but is
NOT a substitute for a coefficient-based reason code framework.

--- METHODOLOGICAL NOTE ---
Both extreme applicants examined have TARGET = 0 — the
"highest risk" applicant did not default. This is not a model
failure. At an 8% base rate, even the riskiest predictions are
usually wrong on any individual case. It illustrates that PD
models RANK POPULATIONS; they do not CLASSIFY INDIVIDUALS.

8. OPEN ITEMS FOR PHASES 9-11
================================================================
1. PROTECTED-ATTRIBUTE EXCLUSION. CODE_GENDER ranking 5th by
   SHAP in the challenger is a fairness exposure. Add an
   explicit exclusion list (gender, and review age, marital
   status, region proxies) as a pipeline step rather than
   relying on IV selection to remove them incidentally.
   Consider a formal fairness audit: approval rate and bad rate
   parity across protected groups.

2. TOP-BAND CAPPING. Publish scores of 630+ as a single band.
   The 620-629 / 630-639 odds reversal is sampling noise on
   151 observations and should not appear in a published table.

3. SCORECARD SIMPLIFICATION (optional). Seven features have
   swings of 4 points or fewer and could be removed with
   negligible performance loss, yielding a 17-feature card.
   Would need re-validation of AUC, KS and monotonicity.

4. DEPLOYMENT ARTEFACTS (Phase 10). The FastAPI service must
   apply, in order: sentinel replacement -> winsorisation
   (train_caps.pkl) -> imputation (train_medians.pkl) -> WoE
   (scorecard_binning.pkl) -> logistic regression
   (scorecard_model.pkl) -> points conversion. Every fitted
   object is persisted; the ORDER is the critical detail.

5. REASON CODE MAPPING TABLE. Formalise the feature-to-
   adverse-action-text mapping used ad hoc in section 6 into a
   lookup that the API can return alongside the score.
